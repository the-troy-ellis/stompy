# The world

**Draft** (2026-11-28): being worked out with the owner. What is decided is
marked so; the rest is proposed. Nothing here is told to the player as a
story: the world is shown (statues, shrines, relics, the way robots act),
and the few words on screen keep the board-book voice.

## Decided

- **Robots, no humans** (the owner). Nothing in the world is human. The
  game is not piloted machines: you play as robots.
- **A robot mythology** (the owner). The big machines, the mechs, are
  **demigods**. The **cities have tiny robots**: about person-sized, in
  streets and buildings at their own small scale, so a demigod 8 to 10 m
  tall stands three storeys over them. Giants stomping round a city is
  literally true, and nobody human is under the foot.
- **Temples summon demigods** (the owner) by offerings: **ore tributes** or
  **salvage sacrifices**. The temple is the base builder's production
  building.

## Proposed

### In one line

The small build the big; the big keep the small.

### Why robots would need a mythology

Myths grow from needs a people cannot meet or explain alone. Tiny robots:

- **wear out** and cannot make big parts, so they need something greater;
- **forget** (resets, failing memory), so know-how leaks away; the rituals
  are maintenance steps nobody understands any more, kept because they work;
- **cannot defend themselves**, so they build protectors;
- **find things they cannot explain**: the dead cities with giant storeys,
  quakes, static storms, lights in the ice.

So they build giants and keep them going with ore and labour, and the
mythology is the half-forgotten manual for summoning, repairing and keeping
a demigod.

### You

The god above. From above you tend your city and its temple (the command
view); when you choose, you descend into one of its demigods and walk the
world through its eyes (the cockpit: the demigod's head, seen from inside,
in 3D).

### Rivals

Other cities with their own temples and demigods, painted their colour
(yours slate blue, theirs mustard). They want the same ore. Their raids
come for your temple; yours go for theirs.

### Summoning

- Tiny robots carry **ore** up the temple steps: the steady supply.
- **Salvage** from fallen demigods, yours or theirs, is hauled home and
  sacrificed: richer than ore, so a battlefield is a field of offerings.
  Parts remember: a fallen demigod's own salvage summons its model faster.
- The summoning takes a few seconds of ritual (a raid can break it), then
  the demigod arrives with a landing you feel and the tiny robots cheer.
- One temple summons any model the city knows; relics from the dead cities
  teach it new ones. Bigger temples summon bigger demigods. Summoning is at
  home only, so the temple is the heart of your territory.

### Places

- **The desert:** ore under the dunes; a long way to see.
- **The glacier:** ore frozen in the ice; slippery, white-outs.
- **The volcanic plain:** the richest ore and the most heat; maybe where
  the first demigods were lit.
- **The forest:** grown over old scrap; close and misty.
- **The dead cities:** from long ago, built at demigod scale (8 m storeys),
  empty: salvage, relics, cover, and maybe something that wakes.

### Shown, not told

Statues of the city's demigods in the squares, made slightly wrong.
Shrines with ore on them. Murals in polygons. Tiny robots that cheer their
own demigod and scatter from the other city's. The system voice stays flat
and dry: "Relic recovered. The faithful are pleased."

## Open

1. **The battles:** demigod against demigod with the tiny robots as crowd
   and workforce, or tiny robots fighting too, in armies underfoot? (It
   sets the big-battle target: tens of giants and thousands of small ones,
   or hundreds of giants.)
2. **Your own tiny robots underfoot:** flattened if you're careless, or
   always scrambling clear?
3. **The construction machines:** is BIG SCOOPER a demigod of digging, with
   the tiny robots doing the small work, or do tiny robots do it all?
4. **Which needs become systems:** power (a Spark deep in the ground,
   beside ore), memory (relics unlock demigods), protection (the loop
   itself)? The rest stays flavour.
5. **Worship:** flavour only, or a resource (devotion) that powers
   summoning?
6. **The tiny robots' manner:** beeping, squabbling characters of their
   own, or a simple crowd?
7. **Salvage vs ore, one temple vs many, summoning time, summoning only at
   home:** the proposals above.
